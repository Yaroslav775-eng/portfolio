Scaling & Profitability

Peak season rescue: how margin-based segmentation and search-intent cleanup saved the BBQ season for a grill brand.

Short version: a Ukrainian BBQ equipment manufacturer and D2C store entered its peak season with a legacy setup — one flat Shopping campaign across 400+ SKUs. After margin-based segmentation, search-intent cleanup and product-layer work, budget moved to profitable items instead of click volume. Season result: €157,642 revenue on €23,217 ad spend, holding 679% ROAS.
Client Ukrainian BBQ equipment manufacturer with a direct-to-consumer online store
Catalog 400+ SKUs: charcoal grills (portable, stationary, with lid), cauldrons, cauldron stoves, wood stoves, skewer sets, accessories
Model In-house manufacturing + own store; 1–3 day delivery across Ukraine
Demand Sharply seasonal — peak demand in spring and summer
Channel Google Ads: Shopping, Performance Max, DSA
Critical flaw Flat Shopping structure that ignored per-product profitability
Strategy Margin-based segmentation, search-intent audit, SKU cleanup, PMax restructuring
Revenue (season) €157,642
Ad spend (season) €23,217
Final ROAS 679%

Why the season started in loss

The store entered its most important sales window with an outdated structure: 400+ SKUs sitting in one flat Shopping campaign. To the algorithm, a €15 grill cleaner and a €2,000 professional smoker looked almost the same.

The outcome was predictable: budget drifted to cheap accessories with high click volume, while the expensive grills that actually generated profit were starved of impressions and funding. Meanwhile, informational traffic — DIY, repairs, recipes — was flowing into the account with no realistic chance of converting.

For a seasonal business this hurts twice: the demand window is short, and every week spent advertising the wrong products never comes back.

The turn: from traffic to profit

Scaling e-commerce is not about raising bids. It is about allocating budget smarter. We dropped the all-in-one approach and rebuilt the account around real business profitability instead of a catalog-wide average ROAS.

Margin-first logic

  • Tier 1: premium segment. Expensive grills moved into dedicated PMax campaigns with higher ROAS targets — they received the bulk of the budget.
  • Tier 2: accessories. Lower-margin items moved to standard Shopping campaigns with more conservative bidding, adding revenue without eating the budget of key categories.
  • Tier 3: long-tail DSA. Dynamic Search Ads captured narrow seasonal queries that classic keyword lists miss.
  • Search-intent hygiene. Commercial demand was separated from informational noise (DIY, repairs, recipes), freeing roughly 15% of budget that used to go to the wrong place.

Surgical work on the assortment

Scaling required more than structure — it required cutting what was dragging the budget down.

Removing dud products. At SKU level we found items that collected clicks but produced no sales. Removing them redirected more than €1,000 per month into positions that actually converted.

Audience signals. We used the strongest search queries as signals for PMax, which accelerated algorithm learning and reached higher-intent audiences faster.

Product layer for the season. Campaign priorities were set by margin and stock — not by traffic volume.

Result: budget working on margin, not volume

At the peak of the season the account was not just spending more — it was spending more precisely. GA4 showed a 45% increase in average order value as focus shifted to premium items. The final window delivered €157k revenue at 6.7x return, and CAC stayed stable despite the aggressive budget ramp.

What we took away from this

  • Catalog-average ROAS lies. With 400+ SKUs in one campaign, a “healthy” average can hide loss-making categories.
  • In seasonal business, structure beats bidding. Raising tROAS is easier than mapping products to margin tiers — and far less effective.
  • Informational traffic is a tax. In a short seasonal window it costs more than in year-round e-commerce.
  • Decisions belong at SKU level, not campaign level — that is where you see what earns and what only collects clicks.
